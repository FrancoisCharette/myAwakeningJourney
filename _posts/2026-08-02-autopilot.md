title: "The autopilot is always running"
categories: [Spirituality, Human Nature]

In the last post I described the two weeks after my motorcycle accident, when my body carried on without me and nobody in my family noticed. I ended with a question I have been circling ever since: how much of an ordinary day is run by that same autopilot, with nobody home and nobody noticing?

I want to take that question seriously here, because I no longer think those two weeks were an exception. I think they were an extreme version of something that happens to everyone, every day.

Everything below is my own interpretation of my own experience. It is hypothesis, not fact, and no one can really confirm or refute it, including me. If you have a better explanation I would genuinely like to read it.

## The signature of a body with nobody in it

There is one detail from those two weeks I keep returning to, because it is the only thing my family noticed at all.

My body answered questions. It did not ask any.

It would reply when spoken to, correctly enough that nobody was alarmed. It never started a conversation, never volunteered anything, never wondered aloud about anything. To my wife and daughter, that looked exactly like a man recovering from a traumatic brain injury, which is a completely reasonable thing to conclude. But looking back, I think it is the clearest description I have of what a body looks like when the Self is not connected to it: **fully responsive, entirely reactive, and initiating nothing.**

Hold on to that, because it is not as unfamiliar as it sounds.

## Every night, the same thing, to everybody

The first feeling I had when I came back after two weeks was the feeling of waking up. Not a strange feeling. Not a mystical one. The ordinary feeling I have had every morning of my life, and that I assume you have too.

That bothered me for a long time, and I now think it is the most important clue I have.

For fifty-eight years I accepted sleep without ever once questioning it. When I happened to watch a documentary on the subject, the experts said sleep is how the body rests and replenishes itself, and that sounded logical, so my Ego accepted it and I moved on.

It no longer satisfies me. Nothing else in the material world replenishes itself by being used less. Every machine we have ever built needs something added back in: plugged into a wall, refueled, recharged. During sleep we eat nothing and drink nothing, the body keeps running and keeps consuming energy, and yet we get up restored. The measurements the sleep researchers make are not wrong. But the explanation built on top of them is an interpretation, and it is incomplete.

My hypothesis is that sleep is the nightly version of what happened to me for two weeks. The Self unplugs from the brain, and the brain resets. Each night we die a little for the night; each morning we are re-born for the day. Sleep is a mini cycle of a life.

Which means the strange thing about my accident is not that my consciousness left. It is that it left for two weeks instead of eight hours, and that my body kept walking around in the meantime.

## The part that should worry us

If the autopilot only ran while we were asleep, this would be an interesting curiosity and nothing more.

But I spent the first fifty-eight years of my life at what I call Level 0 — anchored directly on the Ego axes, fully embedded in the simulation, with no idea that awakening was even a thing and no interest in finding out. I was not unconscious. I went to work. I raised a family. I built a career. By every measure available to anyone watching, including me, I was present.

I now think I was mostly not.

The brain, as far as I can tell, is a receiver and a controller. The controller part is obvious: it runs the body, works the five senses, and navigates the material world. That is the horizontal Ego axes, where time runs in one direction and where every thought you have ever had is located. The receiver part is the vertical Self axes, and almost nobody pays it any attention at all.

Autopilot, in this picture, is simply the controller running while the receiver is disengaged. And there are at least three ways for that to happen. The receiver can be damaged, which is what happened to me for two weeks. It can be unplugged on purpose, which I believe is what sleep is. Or — and this is the one that matters — it can be working perfectly well and simply never tuned in,
which is Level 0, and which can last for decades without anyone noticing. Including you.

## An uncomfortable test

There is a simple way to check where your anchor is, and I recommend it mostly because of how humbling it is.

Try to stop thinking. Not slow down, not think calmer thoughts. Stop.

When I started, I could manage a few seconds. Today, after years of practice, I still have not reached a full minute. I am Level 1, which means I can observe my thoughts and step off the Ego axes briefly, but my anchor is still firmly attached to it. And part of what keeps pulling me back is my own Ego watching my spiritual progress far too closely and interfering with it — what some people call the Spiritual Ego.

I mention my poor numbers deliberately. If a man who has spent over four years actively working at this cannot hold sixty seconds of stillness, then the default state of a human being — the state I lived in for fifty-eight years without once suspecting it — is a controller running a body, generating thought after thought, with the receiver switched off.

That is autopilot. It is not an occasional lapse. It is the baseline.

## Nobody notices, again

Here is what unsettles me most, and it is the same thing that unsettled me about the accident.

For two weeks my family lived with a body that answered questions and initiated nothing, and not one of them detected that I was absent. They had every reason not to. It looked like an ordinary recovery.

The fifty-eight-year version is far better camouflaged than that, because it looks like an ordinary life. Nobody around you notices, because they are running the same way. You do not notice, because noticing is precisely the faculty that is switched off. There is no alarm for this. There is no moment where anyone walks into the room and asks whether you are actually in there.

It took a car running a red light for me to find out.

I am not claiming any of this is proven. It is my interpretation of what I experienced, and I hold it loosely — I expect to revise this page as my understanding changes, which is why it is written down rather than recorded.

But I would ask you to try the sixty seconds before you dismiss it. Not because the result proves my hypothesis, but because whatever you find out about yourself in that minute is yours, and no expert and no teacher and certainly no one on the internet can hand it to you.

Tell me what you found in the comments. Especially if it contradicts me.
